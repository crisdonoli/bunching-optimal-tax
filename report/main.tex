\begin{abstract}
\noindent This report presents the complete solution to Problem Set~2 of Advanced Public Finance (ENECO894), examining behavioral responses to nonlinear income taxation and their welfare implications in Chile. Section~1 replicates the first-kink bunching estimation on administrative \textit{Servicio de Impuestos Internos} (SII) wage-earner records using a fourth-degree polynomial counterfactual with iterative right-tail reweighting, assesses sensitivity across bin widths, fitting windows, and 2005--2023 pooled samples, and discusses the economic interpretation of the estimated elasticity of taxable income ($\hat{\epsilon}_E = 0.2799$) for lowering the exemption threshold. Section~2 formally studies the identifying assumptions of bunching methods by deriving the frictionless uniform-ability benchmark, characterizing attenuation bias under contract-renegotiation frictions ($\phi > 0$), and proposing two nonparametric counterfactual alternatives (a shifted-kink reference distribution and shape-constrained bounded-curvature bounds) that motivate reporting identified intervals rather than point estimates. Section~3 derives the mechanical effect, Pareto-tail behavioral offset, and Marginal Value of Public Funds ($|\mathrm{MVPF}|$) for a proposed $40\% \to 35\%$ top marginal rate reduction, calibrates the economy using Problem Set~1 Pareto tail parameters and top-bracket bunching estimates on synthetic count data, and concludes with a policy brief.
\vspace{20mm}\\

\end{abstract}
